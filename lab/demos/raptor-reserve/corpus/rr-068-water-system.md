# Water System

Category: Infrastructure and logistics
Date: 2026-02-10
Author: Caleb Finch
Source: Raptor Reserve internal record

The island's water comes from a 48 m well near the research station. A pump lifts water to a 12,000 L storage tank on the hill above the buildings. The tank feeds the visitor center, the research station, and the staff cabins by gravity.

The water passes through a sediment filter and a UV unit. The UV lamp is replaced every 12 months. The tank is cleaned every two years. The reserve tests the water monthly for bacteria and every quarter for nitrate and metals.

In 2025 the system used about 1,200 L per day in summer and 300 L per day in winter. There were no boil-water advisories. The pump failed once in July and was repaired in 6 hours with a spare.

The system is drained before 15 November to prevent freezing. The winter caretaker uses a separate 200 L tank in the research station.

The 2026 project replaces the UV unit and adds a second sediment filter. The cost is USD 3,800.

## Overview

Water System is part of the reserve's infrastructure and logistics. The document names the owner, the maintenance schedule, the risks, and the records. The goal is reliable service with a small footprint.

## Specifications

The specifications are in the document. The maintenance lead keeps the current configuration, the warranty papers, and the service log. Any change to the specifications needs approval from the director and the board if the cost is above USD 10,000.

## Maintenance

Preventive maintenance follows the annual calendar. The team logs every inspection, repair, and replacement. Critical spares are kept on the island. The maintenance lead, Caleb Finch, reviews the log at the first Monday meeting.

## Spare parts and tools

The maintenance shed keeps the spare parts that the team needs to keep this part of the operation running: filters, fuses, fasteners, and the parts named in the service manual. Critical parts are ordered before the winter ferry gap. The inventory is checked on the first Monday of each month.

## Risks

The main risks are weather, salt corrosion, wildlife damage, and supply delays. The reserve keeps a storm kit, a spare parts bin, and a contingency line in the budget. Any outage that affects safety is reported to the director the same day.

## Winter

The system is checked before 15 November. The team follows the winter checklist for this system: freeze protection where needed, battery and fuel checks, access marking, and a photo report from the winter caretaker. Any fault is logged and reported to the maintenance lead.

## Records

This part of the operation has a page in the asset register with the installation date, the warranty end date, the service history, and the replacement cost. The register is reviewed every year with the budget.
