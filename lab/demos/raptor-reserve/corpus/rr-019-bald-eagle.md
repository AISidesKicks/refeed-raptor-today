# Bald eagle

Category: Species and ecology
Date: 2026-03-01
Author: Tomas Reed
Source: Raptor Reserve internal record

Bald eagle (Haliaeetus leucocephalus) is a least concern raptor in the reserve's monitoring program. In 2025, the reserve monitored 1 breeding pairs, which fledged 2 chicks. The birds nest in Seal Cove. Their diet is mainly fish and carrion.

The 2025 nest checks used the standard three-visit protocol: occupancy in April, stage and clutch size in May, and chick count in June. Observers stayed at least 150 m from active nests. The pair first nested on the island in 2011.

The main threats are human disturbance at Seal Cove and lead poisoning from scavenged carcasses. The reserve manages them with seasonal trail closures, a ban on lead ammunition, and a 250 m no-fly zone around active nests.

Visitors should watch from the marked viewpoints and keep 100 m from any bird that is alarm-calling or carrying food. If a bird leaves a nest because of a visitor, the visitor should back away slowly and report the encounter to a ranger.

## Field identification

Bald eagle is a very large eagle with a white head and tail in adults. It is most often confused with the osprey, which is smaller and has a dark eye stripe. The scientific name is Haliaeetus leucocephalus. At the reserve, the best clues are the flight silhouette, the wing pattern, and the habitat.

## Habitat and territory

The species nests in Seal Cove. The reserve monitors 1 territories. Observers check the territories from the ground and stay at least 150 m from active nests. The birds use the same territory across years when the site is undisturbed.

## Diet and hunting

The diet is mainly fish and carrion. The birds hunt by soaring and scavenging, and taking fish from the surface. Pellets and prey remains are collected for the diet study (rr-047) when they are found away from nests.

## Breeding timeline

Courtship begins in February. Eggs are laid in March, hatch in April, and the chicks fledge in June. The reserve's three-visit protocol matches this schedule: occupancy in April, stage and clutch size in May, and chick count in June.

## Monitoring results

The 2025 season recorded 1 pairs and 2 fledged chicks. The 2024 season recorded 1 pairs and 2 fledged chicks. The long-term trend is reported in the annual breeding summary (rr-043) and the occupancy analysis (rr-045).

## Threats and management

The lead paragraph lists the main threats. The management response has three parts: seasonal trail closures, a ban on lead ammunition, and a 250 m no-fly zone around active nests. Nest platforms are added where a natural site is unstable. The management plan target is to protect at least 90 percent of occupied territories.

## Visitor guidance

The lead paragraph gives the distance rule. The short version: stay on the marked trail, keep 100 m from a bird that is alarm-calling or carrying food, and do not use playback, drones, or food to get a closer look. Report injured birds to a ranger.
