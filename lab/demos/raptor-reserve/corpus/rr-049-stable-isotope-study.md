# Stable Isotope Study

Category: Research and monitoring
Date: 2026-05-12
Author: Tomas Reed
Source: Raptor Reserve internal record

The stable isotope study uses 40 feather samples from 2025 to separate marine and terrestrial prey in the diet of island raptors. The ratio of carbon and nitrogen isotopes in feathers reflects the food web where the feather grew.

The samples fell into two groups. Ospreys and bald eagles had marine signatures, as expected from a fish diet. Harriers, kestrels, and red-tailed hawks had terrestrial signatures. Peregrines and merlins were intermediate, which fits a diet of shorebirds and songbirds that move between habitats.

The method cannot identify prey species. It can only separate broad habitat groups. The reserve uses it as a check on the pellet study, not as a replacement. The sample is also biased toward birds that molted on the island.

The study cost USD 4,800 and was funded by the friends group. The full isotope table is in the annual data release. The 2026 plan is to add 20 samples from the mainland migration station to compare local and migrant birds.

## Background

Stable Isotope Study is part of the reserve's long-term monitoring program. The question is whether the island's raptor population is changing, and whether the change is driven by weather, prey, disease, or human disturbance. The reserve has collected comparable data since 2016.

## Methods

The analysis uses the standard field protocols. Counts use six fixed stations and a four-hour window. Nest checks use three visits and a 150 m observation distance. Occupancy models use the 2016-2025 records and account for detection probability. The data manager checks the records before analysis.

## Results

The headline numbers are in the document. The full tables are in the annual data release. Estimates are reported with 95 percent confidence intervals. A difference is treated as meaningful only when the intervals do not overlap and the field effort was similar.

## What changed since the last report

The previous report used the same routes and the same three-visit protocol, so the comparison is direct. The 2026 analysis adds the new South Beach boardwalk data and the two acoustic stations installed in 2025. The older records are flagged where the effort or the observer changed. A difference that comes from a new route is not treated as a population change.

## Discussion

The reserve reads the results against the management plan. A single year is not a trend. A change that appears in one species but not in its prey or habitat is treated with caution. The team also checks whether a new observer, a new route, or a new sensor could explain the pattern.
