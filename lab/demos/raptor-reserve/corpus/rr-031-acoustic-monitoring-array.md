# Acoustic Monitoring Array

Category: Field operations
Date: 2026-04-10
Author: Tomas Reed
Source: Raptor Reserve internal record

The acoustic array has 12 stations: four at Heron Marsh, three at Blacktail Forest, two at North Point, two at South Beach, and one at Granite Ridge. Each station records 24 hours a day during the breeding season and 12 hours a day in winter.

Staff swap SD cards and batteries every two weeks. The recorders are mounted 2 to 4 m above the ground, away from the surf and the generator. The microphones are omnidirectional and have a usable range of about 300 m in open habitat.

The 2025 season produced 8,200 hours of audio. The classifier flagged 1,840 raptor calls. A human reviewer checked a 10 percent sample and found 92 percent precision and 78 percent recall. The main confusion was between merlin and American kestrel calls.

Acoustic data is stored as FLAC files with a date and station id. The classifier output is stored in a separate table so the raw audio can be reanalyzed with a better model. The reserve keeps raw audio for five years.

The array is not a replacement for the migration count. It is a way to detect cryptic species and to measure activity at night.

## Purpose and scope

Acoustic Monitoring Array is the standard procedure for this part of the field program. It applies to staff, seasonal employees, and trained volunteers. It does not replace the safety plan (rr-005) or the emergency response plan (rr-006).

## Roles

The head ranger, Elin Ward, owns the procedure. The field lead assigns teams and checks the weather. The data manager, Samir Haddad, checks the records. The veterinarian, Dr. Priya Nair, is consulted for any wildlife handling.

## Procedure

1. Check the weather, the tide, and the radio.
2. Sign out at the research station and record the team.
3. Follow the route and the timing in the document.
4. Record the data in the field form before leaving the site.
5. Report any safety issue, injury, or unusual wildlife behavior to the head ranger.
6. Sign in at the end of the shift.

## Data and records

Records go into the field database within 24 hours. Each record needs date, time, observer, location, weather, and a confidence note. Photos are optional and should not include people without consent. The data manager checks the records before the weekly summary.

## Safety

Work in pairs. Carry a radio, a first aid kit, and water. Stay 150 m from the cliff edge at North Point. Do not fly a drone above 28 km/h wind or within 250 m of an active nest. Stop work in lightning, fog below 2 km visibility, or a red risk level.

## Why it matters

Consistent records are the only way to compare years. A missed field or a changed route can look like a population change. The reserve would rather lose a day of data than publish a number that cannot be defended.

## Review

The procedure is reviewed before each field season. Changes are tested in a drill and then added to the next version. The current version took effect on 2026-04-10.
