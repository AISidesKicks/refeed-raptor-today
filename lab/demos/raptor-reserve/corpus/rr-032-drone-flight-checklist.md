# Drone Flight Checklist

Category: Field operations
Date: 2026-05-01
Author: Elin Ward
Source: Raptor Reserve internal record

Drone flights are used for nest surveys, shoreline mapping, and search and rescue. Every flight needs approval from the head ranger and a written flight plan.

The pilot checks the weather first. Flights are cancelled at 28 km/h wind, visibility below 2 km, rain, snow, or lightning within 10 km. The drone must stay within 3.5 km of the pilot and above 30 m except during takeoff and landing.

The battery must be at least 80 percent at takeoff. The planned flight time cannot exceed 24 minutes, which leaves a 30 percent reserve. The pilot does not fly over people, boats, or the visitor center.

No flight is allowed within 250 m of an active nest during the breeding season. The pilot checks the nest map before takeoff and logs the flight in the drone book. The log records date, pilot, purpose, weather, max altitude, duration, battery used, and any wildlife response.

If the drone loses link, the pilot switches to return-to-home and clears the landing zone. If the drone does not return, the pilot marks the last known point and calls the head ranger.

## Purpose and scope

Drone Flight Checklist is the standard procedure for this part of the field program. It applies to staff, seasonal employees, and trained volunteers. It does not replace the safety plan (rr-005) or the emergency response plan (rr-006).

## Roles

The head ranger, Elin Ward, owns the procedure. The field lead assigns teams and checks the weather. The data manager, Samir Haddad, checks the records. The veterinarian, Dr. Priya Nair, is consulted for any wildlife handling.

## Procedure

1. Check the weather, the tide, and the radio.
2. Sign out at the research station and record the team.
3. Follow the route and the timing in the document.
4. Record the data in the field form before leaving the site.
5. Report any safety issue, injury, or unusual wildlife behavior to the head ranger.
6. Sign in at the end of the shift.

## Data and records

Records go into the field database within 24 hours. Each record needs date, time, observer, location, weather, and a confidence note. Photos are optional and should not include people without consent. The data manager checks the records before the weekly summary.

## Safety

Work in pairs. Carry a radio, a first aid kit, and water. Stay 150 m from the cliff edge at North Point. Do not fly a drone above 28 km/h wind or within 250 m of an active nest. Stop work in lightning, fog below 2 km visibility, or a red risk level.

## Why it matters

Consistent records are the only way to compare years. A missed field or a changed route can look like a population change. The reserve would rather lose a day of data than publish a number that cannot be defended.

## Review

The procedure is reviewed before each field season. Changes are tested in a drill and then added to the next version. The current version took effect on 2026-05-01.
