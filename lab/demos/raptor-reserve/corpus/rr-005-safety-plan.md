# Safety Plan

Category: Governance
Date: 2026-02-15
Author: Elin Ward
Source: Raptor Reserve internal record

The safety plan uses three risk levels. Green means normal work. Amber means limited work, with a buddy and a radio check every hour. Red means no field work except emergency response.

Weather limits are specific. Drone flights stop at 28 km/h wind, visibility below 2 km, or any lightning within 10 km. Staff stay at least 150 m from the cliff edge at North Point unless they are clipped in. Life jackets are required on all boats, including the 3.5 km crossing at The Narrows.

Field teams carry a first aid kit, a satellite messenger, and a VHF radio. The reserve has two automated external defibrillators, one at the visitor center and one at the research station. All staff are trained in CPR and wilderness first aid every two years.

Any injury, near miss, or equipment failure is reported within 24 hours. The safety committee reviews reports on the first Monday of each month. The committee can close a trail, a building, or a program until the risk is fixed.

The safety plan is tested in two drills each year: one lost-visitor drill in May and one fire drill in October.

## Scope

Safety Plan applies to the board, the director, full-time and seasonal staff, volunteers, researchers, and contractors working on Blacktail Island. It does not replace the charter (rr-001), the emergency response plan (rr-006), or the safety plan (rr-005). When two documents disagree, the charter wins, then the management plan, then the specific policy.

## Roles and responsibilities

The board approves the document. The director, Dr. Mara Voss, owns implementation and reports exceptions to the board. The head ranger, Elin Ward, is the day-to-day contact for field staff. The data manager, Samir Haddad, keeps the signed copy in the document register and makes sure the shared drive has the current version.

## What staff should do

Read the document during onboarding. Ask the director or the head ranger if a situation is not covered. Record decisions that deviate from the document, including the reason and the date. Do not edit the shared copy. Send proposed changes to the data manager.

## Records and evidence

The reserve keeps the approved version, the board minutes, the public comment summary, and the annual review memo. Records are kept for 10 years. Personnel details are stored separately and are not part of the public corpus.

## Review and amendment

Review follows the schedule in the document. If the document does not set one, the default is every two years. Proposed changes go to the board packet 14 days before the meeting. A charter change needs a two-thirds vote; other documents need a simple majority.

## Common failure modes

The most common problems are outdated copies, unclear ownership, and field decisions that are never written down. The reserve fixes these by naming one owner, using a single document register, and reviewing the field log at the Monday safety meeting.
