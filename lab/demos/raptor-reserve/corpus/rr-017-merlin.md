# Merlin

Category: Species and ecology
Date: 2026-03-01
Author: Tomas Reed
Source: Raptor Reserve internal record

Merlin (Falco columbarius) is a state special concern raptor in the reserve's monitoring program. In 2025, the reserve monitored 2 breeding pairs, which fledged 3 chicks. The birds nest in the coastal spruce stands. Their diet is mainly songbirds.

The 2025 nest checks used the standard three-visit protocol: occupancy in April, stage and clutch size in May, and chick count in June. Observers stayed at least 150 m from active nests. Merlins arrived as breeders in 2012.

The main threats are disturbance of coastal spruce nests and predation by raccoons. The reserve manages them with seasonal trail closures, a ban on lead ammunition, and a 250 m no-fly zone around active nests.

Visitors should watch from the marked viewpoints and keep 100 m from any bird that is alarm-calling or carrying food. If a bird leaves a nest because of a visitor, the visitor should back away slowly and report the encounter to a ranger.

## Field identification

Merlin is a small, compact falcon with pointed wings and a streaked chest. It is most often confused with the American kestrel, which is more colorful and hovers more often. The scientific name is Falco columbarius. At the reserve, the best clues are the flight silhouette, the wing pattern, and the habitat.

## Habitat and territory

The species nests in the coastal spruce stands. The reserve monitors 2 territories. Observers check the territories from the ground and stay at least 150 m from active nests. The birds use the same territory across years when the site is undisturbed.

## Diet and hunting

The diet is mainly songbirds. The birds hunt by fast pursuit of songbirds in open air. Pellets and prey remains are collected for the diet study (rr-047) when they are found away from nests.

## Breeding timeline

Pairs return in April. Eggs are laid in May, hatch in June, and the chicks fledge in July. The reserve's three-visit protocol matches this schedule: occupancy in April, stage and clutch size in May, and chick count in June.

## Monitoring results

The 2025 season recorded 2 pairs and 3 fledged chicks. The 2024 season recorded 1 pairs and 2 fledged chicks. The long-term trend is reported in the annual breeding summary (rr-043) and the occupancy analysis (rr-045).

## Threats and management

The lead paragraph lists the main threats. The management response has three parts: seasonal trail closures, a ban on lead ammunition, and a 250 m no-fly zone around active nests. Nest platforms are added where a natural site is unstable. The management plan target is to protect at least 90 percent of occupied territories.

## Visitor guidance

The lead paragraph gives the distance rule. The short version: stay on the marked trail, keep 100 m from a bird that is alarm-calling or carrying food, and do not use playback, drones, or food to get a closer look. Report injured birds to a ranger.
