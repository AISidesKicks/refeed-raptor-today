# Fuel Storage Inspection

Category: Field operations
Date: 2026-06-14
Author: Caleb Finch
Source: Raptor Reserve internal record

The reserve stores fuel in a fenced compound behind the maintenance shed. The inventory on 14 June 2026 was 1,200 L of diesel and 400 L of gasoline. The diesel tank is double-walled and sits on a concrete pad. The gasoline is stored in four 100 L portable tanks.

The inspection checked tanks, hoses, valves, labels, secondary containment, fire extinguishers, and signage. There were no leaks. Two minor issues were found: a cracked hose on the diesel pump and a missing "no smoking" sign on the gate. Both were fixed the same day.

The compound has a 4,000 L containment curb and a drain that can be closed. Spill kits are stored at the gate and in the maintenance truck. Staff are trained to stop the source, contain the spill, and call the state spill line.

The next inspection is in December. The reserve does not store fuel at North Point or Heron Marsh; all field equipment is refueled at the main compound.

## Purpose and scope

Fuel Storage Inspection is the standard procedure for this part of the field program. It applies to staff, seasonal employees, and trained volunteers. It does not replace the safety plan (rr-005) or the emergency response plan (rr-006).

## Roles

The head ranger, Elin Ward, owns the procedure. The field lead assigns teams and checks the weather. The data manager, Samir Haddad, checks the records. The veterinarian, Dr. Priya Nair, is consulted for any wildlife handling.

## Procedure

1. Check the weather, the tide, and the radio.
2. Sign out at the research station and record the team.
3. Follow the route and the timing in the document.
4. Record the data in the field form before leaving the site.
5. Report any safety issue, injury, or unusual wildlife behavior to the head ranger.
6. Sign in at the end of the shift.

## Data and records

Records go into the field database within 24 hours. Each record needs date, time, observer, location, weather, and a confidence note. Photos are optional and should not include people without consent. The data manager checks the records before the weekly summary.

## Safety

Work in pairs. Carry a radio, a first aid kit, and water. Stay 150 m from the cliff edge at North Point. Do not fly a drone above 28 km/h wind or within 250 m of an active nest. Stop work in lightning, fog below 2 km visibility, or a red risk level.

## Why it matters

Consistent records are the only way to compare years. A missed field or a changed route can look like a population change. The reserve would rather lose a day of data than publish a number that cannot be defended.

## Review

The procedure is reviewed before each field season. Changes are tested in a drill and then added to the next version. The current version took effect on 2026-06-14.
