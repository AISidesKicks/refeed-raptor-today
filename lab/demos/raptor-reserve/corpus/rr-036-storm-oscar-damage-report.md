# Storm Oscar Damage Report

Category: Field operations
Date: 2026-01-22
Author: Caleb Finch
Source: Raptor Reserve internal record

Storm Oscar reached Blacktail Island on 2026-01-18. The weather station recorded a peak gust of 118 km/h at 03:40. Rainfall was 41 mm in 12 hours. The ferry was cancelled for two days.

The South Beach boardwalk lost 42 m of decking and two support posts. The visitor center roof lost six shingles. The North Point lighthouse lost a window pane, and the old boathouse door was pushed in. No one was injured.

The reserve closed South Beach and the lower boardwalk on 18 January. The maintenance team rebuilt the boardwalk in six weeks. The repair cost USD 18,400, covered by the capital budget and a USD 5,000 donation from the friends group. The new decking uses a higher grade of Marine Board and stainless fasteners.

The storm also moved the South Beach dune line by about 3 m. The reserve added the change to the shoreline monitoring dataset. The next survey will check whether the least tern nesting area is still above the spring high tide line.

The boardwalk reopened on 1 March 2026.

## Purpose and scope

Storm Oscar Damage Report is the standard procedure for this part of the field program. It applies to staff, seasonal employees, and trained volunteers. It does not replace the safety plan (rr-005) or the emergency response plan (rr-006).

## Roles

The head ranger, Elin Ward, owns the procedure. The field lead assigns teams and checks the weather. The data manager, Samir Haddad, checks the records. The veterinarian, Dr. Priya Nair, is consulted for any wildlife handling.

## Procedure

1. Check the weather, the tide, and the radio.
2. Sign out at the research station and record the team.
3. Follow the route and the timing in the document.
4. Record the data in the field form before leaving the site.
5. Report any safety issue, injury, or unusual wildlife behavior to the head ranger.
6. Sign in at the end of the shift.

## Data and records

Records go into the field database within 24 hours. Each record needs date, time, observer, location, weather, and a confidence note. Photos are optional and should not include people without consent. The data manager checks the records before the weekly summary.

## Safety

Work in pairs. Carry a radio, a first aid kit, and water. Stay 150 m from the cliff edge at North Point. Do not fly a drone above 28 km/h wind or within 250 m of an active nest. Stop work in lightning, fog below 2 km visibility, or a red risk level.

## Why it matters

Consistent records are the only way to compare years. A missed field or a changed route can look like a population change. The reserve would rather lose a day of data than publish a number that cannot be defended.

## Review

The procedure is reviewed before each field season. Changes are tested in a drill and then added to the next version. The current version took effect on 2026-01-22.
