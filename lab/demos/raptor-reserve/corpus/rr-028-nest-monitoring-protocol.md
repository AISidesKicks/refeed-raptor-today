# Nest Monitoring Protocol

Category: Field operations
Date: 2026-03-15
Author: Tomas Reed
Source: Raptor Reserve internal record

Each raptor territory gets three visits. The first visit in April checks occupancy and nest building. The second visit in May records stage, clutch size, and incubation. The third visit in June counts chicks and estimates fledge date.

Observers stay at least 150 m from active nests. They use binoculars or a spotting scope, not a drone. If a bird alarm-calls or leaves the nest, the observer backs away and records the disturbance. A nest is not approached again for at least 48 hours.

The field form records territory id, date, time, observer, weather, distance, nest substrate, stage, adult behavior, chick count, and any threats. Photos are taken from the observation point, not from the nest.

A territory is occupied if an adult is seen in suitable habitat during the breeding season, or if a nest is active. A nest is successful if at least one chick fledges. The reserve does not count a nest as successful if the chicks disappeared before fledging, even if the cause is unknown.

Data is entered within 24 hours. The ecologist reviews all records before the annual breeding report.

## Purpose and scope

Nest Monitoring Protocol is the standard procedure for this part of the field program. It applies to staff, seasonal employees, and trained volunteers. It does not replace the safety plan (rr-005) or the emergency response plan (rr-006).

## Roles

The head ranger, Elin Ward, owns the procedure. The field lead assigns teams and checks the weather. The data manager, Samir Haddad, checks the records. The veterinarian, Dr. Priya Nair, is consulted for any wildlife handling.

## Procedure

1. Check the weather, the tide, and the radio.
2. Sign out at the research station and record the team.
3. Follow the route and the timing in the document.
4. Record the data in the field form before leaving the site.
5. Report any safety issue, injury, or unusual wildlife behavior to the head ranger.
6. Sign in at the end of the shift.

## Data and records

Records go into the field database within 24 hours. Each record needs date, time, observer, location, weather, and a confidence note. Photos are optional and should not include people without consent. The data manager checks the records before the weekly summary.

## Safety

Work in pairs. Carry a radio, a first aid kit, and water. Stay 150 m from the cliff edge at North Point. Do not fly a drone above 28 km/h wind or within 250 m of an active nest. Stop work in lightning, fog below 2 km visibility, or a red risk level.

## Why it matters

Consistent records are the only way to compare years. A missed field or a changed route can look like a population change. The reserve would rather lose a day of data than publish a number that cannot be defended.

## Review

The procedure is reviewed before each field season. Changes are tested in a drill and then added to the next version. The current version took effect on 2026-03-15.
