# Fall Migration Count Protocol

Category: Field operations
Date: 2026-08-01
Author: Elin Ward
Source: Raptor Reserve internal record

The fall migration count runs from 15 August to 15 November. The season is longer than spring because juvenile birds move later and the weather is more variable. The reserve counts on five days each week, with a fixed Saturday hawk watch at Granite Ridge.

The six spring stations are used again. The Granite Ridge station is the most productive in September and October, when northwest winds push birds along the ridge. The Seal Cove station is better in August, when ospreys and young harriers are still moving.

Observers record the same fields as the spring count. The fall count also records flock size for sharp-shinned hawks and broad-winged hawks, because those species often move in groups. A flock is counted as one observation with a group size, not as separate entries.

The 2025 season lost 11 count days to fog, rain, or high wind. The 2026 target is to lose no more than 9 days. The coordinator can add a make-up count on a Sunday if a front passes.

The fall report is due by 15 December.

## Purpose and scope

Fall Migration Count Protocol is the standard procedure for this part of the field program. It applies to staff, seasonal employees, and trained volunteers. It does not replace the safety plan (rr-005) or the emergency response plan (rr-006).

## Roles

The head ranger, Elin Ward, owns the procedure. The field lead assigns teams and checks the weather. The data manager, Samir Haddad, checks the records. The veterinarian, Dr. Priya Nair, is consulted for any wildlife handling.

## Procedure

1. Check the weather, the tide, and the radio.
2. Sign out at the research station and record the team.
3. Follow the route and the timing in the document.
4. Record the data in the field form before leaving the site.
5. Report any safety issue, injury, or unusual wildlife behavior to the head ranger.
6. Sign in at the end of the shift.

## Data and records

Records go into the field database within 24 hours. Each record needs date, time, observer, location, weather, and a confidence note. Photos are optional and should not include people without consent. The data manager checks the records before the weekly summary.

## Safety

Work in pairs. Carry a radio, a first aid kit, and water. Stay 150 m from the cliff edge at North Point. Do not fly a drone above 28 km/h wind or within 250 m of an active nest. Stop work in lightning, fog below 2 km visibility, or a red risk level.

## Why it matters

Consistent records are the only way to compare years. A missed field or a changed route can look like a population change. The reserve would rather lose a day of data than publish a number that cannot be defended.

## Review

The procedure is reviewed before each field season. Changes are tested in a drill and then added to the next version. The current version took effect on 2026-08-01.
