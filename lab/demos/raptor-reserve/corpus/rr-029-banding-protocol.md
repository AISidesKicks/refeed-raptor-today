# Banding Protocol

Category: Field operations
Date: 2026-05-20
Author: Tomas Reed
Source: Raptor Reserve internal record

The reserve bands raptor chicks in June, when the birds are large enough for a USGS leg band but not yet able to fly. Two licensed banders work together. At least one staff member watches the nest and keeps the adults at a distance.

The team targets 30 chicks across six species: osprey, red-tailed hawk, northern harrier, American kestrel, barred owl, and peregrine falcon. The team does not band every nest; the goal is a representative sample, not a complete inventory.

Each bird gets a numbered USGS band and a color band with a two-letter code. The team records species, age, sex if known, weight, wing chord, bill length, body condition, and ectoparasites. Handling time is kept under 20 minutes per bird.

Equipment is disinfected between nests. Banders wear gloves and eye protection. If a chick is injured or severely underweight, the team stops and calls the veterinarian.

Data goes to the USGS Bird Banding Laboratory and to the reserve database. Resighting reports from visitors are welcome and should include the color code, date, and location.

## Purpose and scope

Banding Protocol is the standard procedure for this part of the field program. It applies to staff, seasonal employees, and trained volunteers. It does not replace the safety plan (rr-005) or the emergency response plan (rr-006).

## Roles

The head ranger, Elin Ward, owns the procedure. The field lead assigns teams and checks the weather. The data manager, Samir Haddad, checks the records. The veterinarian, Dr. Priya Nair, is consulted for any wildlife handling.

## Procedure

1. Check the weather, the tide, and the radio.
2. Sign out at the research station and record the team.
3. Follow the route and the timing in the document.
4. Record the data in the field form before leaving the site.
5. Report any safety issue, injury, or unusual wildlife behavior to the head ranger.
6. Sign in at the end of the shift.

## Data and records

Records go into the field database within 24 hours. Each record needs date, time, observer, location, weather, and a confidence note. Photos are optional and should not include people without consent. The data manager checks the records before the weekly summary.

## Safety

Work in pairs. Carry a radio, a first aid kit, and water. Stay 150 m from the cliff edge at North Point. Do not fly a drone above 28 km/h wind or within 250 m of an active nest. Stop work in lightning, fog below 2 km visibility, or a red risk level.

## Why it matters

Consistent records are the only way to compare years. A missed field or a changed route can look like a population change. The reserve would rather lose a day of data than publish a number that cannot be defended.

## Review

The procedure is reviewed before each field season. Changes are tested in a drill and then added to the next version. The current version took effect on 2026-05-20.
