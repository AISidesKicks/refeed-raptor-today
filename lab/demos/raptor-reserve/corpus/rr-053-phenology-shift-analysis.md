# Phenology Shift Analysis

Category: Research and monitoring
Date: 2026-07-01
Author: Tomas Reed
Source: Raptor Reserve internal record

The reserve analyzed 10 years of first-arrival and last-departure dates for 14 migratory raptor species. Spring arrival is now 5 days earlier than in 2016. Fall departure is 3 days later. The changes are small but consistent.

Ospreys and northern harriers show the strongest spring shift. Merlins and sharp-shinned hawks show the weakest. The pattern matches warmer spring water and earlier snowmelt on the mainland, but the island data alone cannot prove the cause.

The analysis uses the first confirmed sighting of each species at a count station. Observations are filtered for unusual early records, which are checked against photos or a second observer. The trend is estimated with a simple linear model and a 95 percent confidence interval.

A 5-day shift can matter. If prey peaks before chicks hatch, the nest can fail. The reserve will compare the phenology data with the prey survey and the breeding dates in 2027.

The full species table is in the annual data release.

## Background

Phenology Shift Analysis is part of the reserve's long-term monitoring program. The question is whether the island's raptor population is changing, and whether the change is driven by weather, prey, disease, or human disturbance. The reserve has collected comparable data since 2016.

## Methods

The analysis uses the standard field protocols. Counts use six fixed stations and a four-hour window. Nest checks use three visits and a 150 m observation distance. Occupancy models use the 2016-2025 records and account for detection probability. The data manager checks the records before analysis.

## Results

The headline numbers are in the document. The full tables are in the annual data release. Estimates are reported with 95 percent confidence intervals. A difference is treated as meaningful only when the intervals do not overlap and the field effort was similar.

## What changed since the last report

The previous report used the same routes and the same three-visit protocol, so the comparison is direct. The 2026 analysis adds the new South Beach boardwalk data and the two acoustic stations installed in 2025. The older records are flagged where the effort or the observer changed. A difference that comes from a new route is not treated as a population change.

## Discussion

The reserve reads the results against the management plan. A single year is not a trend. A change that appears in one species but not in its prey or habitat is treated with caution. The team also checks whether a new observer, a new route, or a new sensor could explain the pattern.
