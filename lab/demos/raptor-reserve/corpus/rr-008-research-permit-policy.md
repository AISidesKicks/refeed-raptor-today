# Research Permit Policy

Category: Governance
Date: 2025-03-01
Author: Tomas Reed
Source: Raptor Reserve internal record

Any research on Blacktail Island needs a permit. The application asks for the research question, methods, locations, dates, equipment, and the names of everyone who will be on the island. Applications are due at least 45 days before the field season.

Permit fees are waived for students, USD 150 for nonprofit researchers, and USD 400 for commercial researchers. The reserve limits the island to six active projects at a time so the birds are not disturbed.

Researchers must share raw data with the reserve within 90 days of the field season. They must submit an annual report and a plain-language summary. The reserve gets two copies of any publication, and the researcher must acknowledge the reserve in the paper.

Work within 250 m of an active nest needs a separate review by the veterinarian and the head ranger. Work that involves capturing, banding, or sampling birds needs federal and state permits in addition to the reserve permit.

Permits can be suspended for repeated disturbance, missing data, or unsafe behavior. The suspension is reviewed by the research committee.

## Scope

Research Permit Policy applies to the board, the director, full-time and seasonal staff, volunteers, researchers, and contractors working on Blacktail Island. It does not replace the charter (rr-001), the emergency response plan (rr-006), or the safety plan (rr-005). When two documents disagree, the charter wins, then the management plan, then the specific policy.

## Roles and responsibilities

The board approves the document. The director, Dr. Mara Voss, owns implementation and reports exceptions to the board. The head ranger, Elin Ward, is the day-to-day contact for field staff. The data manager, Samir Haddad, keeps the signed copy in the document register and makes sure the shared drive has the current version.

## What staff should do

Read the document during onboarding. Ask the director or the head ranger if a situation is not covered. Record decisions that deviate from the document, including the reason and the date. Do not edit the shared copy. Send proposed changes to the data manager.

## Records and evidence

The reserve keeps the approved version, the board minutes, the public comment summary, and the annual review memo. Records are kept for 10 years. Personnel details are stored separately and are not part of the public corpus.

## Review and amendment

Review follows the schedule in the document. If the document does not set one, the default is every two years. Proposed changes go to the board packet 14 days before the meeting. A charter change needs a two-thirds vote; other documents need a simple majority.

## Common failure modes

The most common problems are outdated copies, unclear ownership, and field decisions that are never written down. The reserve fixes these by naming one owner, using a single document register, and reviewing the field log at the Monday safety meeting.
