# Partnership with the Maine Coast Trust

Category: Governance
Date: 2024-06-01
Author: Dr. Mara Voss
Source: Raptor Reserve internal record

Raptor Reserve and the Maine Coast Trust signed a 20-year partnership agreement in 2024. The agreement runs from 2024 to 2044 and focuses on coastal habitat, water quality, and shared monitoring.

The trust provides USD 250,000 over the first five years. It also funds two shared staff positions: a coastal ecologist and a community engagement coordinator. The reserve provides field sites, housing, and access to its long-term datasets.

Both organizations use the same monitoring protocols for water temperature, salinity, and shoreline change. Data is shared through the reserve's open data portal. Either partner can publish a summary, but the other partner gets 30 days to review it.

The agreement can be renewed for another 10 years by mutual consent. Either partner can exit with 12 months' notice. The first annual review took place in June 2025 and found that the shared monitoring had already produced one peer-reviewed paper.

The partnership does not transfer land ownership. The reserve remains the sole owner of the island.

## Scope

Partnership with the Maine Coast Trust applies to the board, the director, full-time and seasonal staff, volunteers, researchers, and contractors working on Blacktail Island. It does not replace the charter (rr-001), the emergency response plan (rr-006), or the safety plan (rr-005). When two documents disagree, the charter wins, then the management plan, then the specific policy.

## Roles and responsibilities

The board approves the document. The director, Dr. Mara Voss, owns implementation and reports exceptions to the board. The head ranger, Elin Ward, is the day-to-day contact for field staff. The data manager, Samir Haddad, keeps the signed copy in the document register and makes sure the shared drive has the current version.

## What staff should do

Read the document during onboarding. Ask the director or the head ranger if a situation is not covered. Record decisions that deviate from the document, including the reason and the date. Do not edit the shared copy. Send proposed changes to the data manager.

## Records and evidence

The reserve keeps the approved version, the board minutes, the public comment summary, and the annual review memo. Records are kept for 10 years. Personnel details are stored separately and are not part of the public corpus.

## Review and amendment

Review follows the schedule in the document. If the document does not set one, the default is every two years. Proposed changes go to the board packet 14 days before the meeting. A charter change needs a two-thirds vote; other documents need a simple majority.

## Common failure modes

The most common problems are outdated copies, unclear ownership, and field decisions that are never written down. The reserve fixes these by naming one owner, using a single document register, and reviewing the field log at the Monday safety meeting.
