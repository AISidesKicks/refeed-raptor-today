# Patrol Log, 12 September 2026

Category: Field operations
Date: 2026-09-12
Author: Elin Ward
Source: Raptor Reserve internal record

Observer: Elin Ward, head ranger.
Route: North Point to Heron Marsh, via the west trail and the lower boardwalk.
Time: 06:40 to 11:20.
Weather: fog at dawn, clearing by 09:00. Wind 14 km/h from the northwest. Visibility 1.2 km at 07:00, 8 km by 10:00. High tide 08:12.

At 07:05 I checked the North Point closure sign. The peregrine pair was on the cliff, no alarm calls. At 07:40 I found fresh osprey sticks on the platform at the Heron Marsh south nest. Two adults were circling, one carrying a fish.

At 08:20 I met four visitors near the lower boardwalk. They stayed on the trail and asked about the drone. I explained the 250 m nest rule and the 28 km/h wind limit. The wind was 14 km/h, so I approved the 09:30 drone flight over the marsh for the nest survey. I logged the approval in the drone book.

At 10:05 I checked the boardwalk repair from storm Oscar. The new decking is holding. One handrail bolt was loose; I tightened it and flagged the rail for the maintenance team.

No injured birds. No off-trail tracks. No dogs.

## Purpose and scope

Patrol Log, 12 September 2026 is the standard procedure for this part of the field program. It applies to staff, seasonal employees, and trained volunteers. It does not replace the safety plan (rr-005) or the emergency response plan (rr-006).

## Roles

The head ranger, Elin Ward, owns the procedure. The field lead assigns teams and checks the weather. The data manager, Samir Haddad, checks the records. The veterinarian, Dr. Priya Nair, is consulted for any wildlife handling.

## Procedure

1. Check the weather, the tide, and the radio.
2. Sign out at the research station and record the team.
3. Follow the route and the timing in the document.
4. Record the data in the field form before leaving the site.
5. Report any safety issue, injury, or unusual wildlife behavior to the head ranger.
6. Sign in at the end of the shift.

## Data and records

Records go into the field database within 24 hours. Each record needs date, time, observer, location, weather, and a confidence note. Photos are optional and should not include people without consent. The data manager checks the records before the weekly summary.

## Safety

Work in pairs. Carry a radio, a first aid kit, and water. Stay 150 m from the cliff edge at North Point. Do not fly a drone above 28 km/h wind or within 250 m of an active nest. Stop work in lightning, fog below 2 km visibility, or a red risk level.

## Why it matters

Consistent records are the only way to compare years. A missed field or a changed route can look like a population change. The reserve would rather lose a day of data than publish a number that cannot be defended.

## Review

The procedure is reviewed before each field season. Changes are tested in a drill and then added to the next version. The current version took effect on 2026-09-12.
