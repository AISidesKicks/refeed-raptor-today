# Lead Poisoning Case: Juniper

Category: Field operations
Date: 2024-10-25
Author: Dr. Priya Nair
Source: Raptor Reserve internal record

On 2024-10-09, a visitor found an adult female peregrine on the ground near North Point. The bird was weak, had drooping wings, and could not stand. The ranger team captured her with a net and brought her to the clinic.

The bird was known as Juniper, the female of the North Point pair. Her blood lead level was 0.42 ppm. The threshold for clinical concern is 0.2 ppm. She was started on chelation therapy, fluids, and a high-energy diet.

Juniper improved slowly. She gained weight in the first week, began standing in the second week, and was flying in the flight pen by day 14. She was treated for 18 days and released on 27 October 2024.

The total cost was USD 6,200. The source of the lead was never confirmed, but a spent rifle bullet was found in a deer carcass 1.8 km from the territory. The reserve banned lead ammunition in 2019, but the ban does not cover neighboring land.

The case is used in the lead monitoring program and in the visitor center exhibit.

## Purpose and scope

Lead Poisoning Case: Juniper is the standard procedure for this part of the field program. It applies to staff, seasonal employees, and trained volunteers. It does not replace the safety plan (rr-005) or the emergency response plan (rr-006).

## Roles

The head ranger, Elin Ward, owns the procedure. The field lead assigns teams and checks the weather. The data manager, Samir Haddad, checks the records. The veterinarian, Dr. Priya Nair, is consulted for any wildlife handling.

## Procedure

1. Check the weather, the tide, and the radio.
2. Sign out at the research station and record the team.
3. Follow the route and the timing in the document.
4. Record the data in the field form before leaving the site.
5. Report any safety issue, injury, or unusual wildlife behavior to the head ranger.
6. Sign in at the end of the shift.

## Data and records

Records go into the field database within 24 hours. Each record needs date, time, observer, location, weather, and a confidence note. Photos are optional and should not include people without consent. The data manager checks the records before the weekly summary.

## Safety

Work in pairs. Carry a radio, a first aid kit, and water. Stay 150 m from the cliff edge at North Point. Do not fly a drone above 28 km/h wind or within 250 m of an active nest. Stop work in lightning, fog below 2 km visibility, or a red risk level.

## Why it matters

Consistent records are the only way to compare years. A missed field or a changed route can look like a population change. The reserve would rather lose a day of data than publish a number that cannot be defended.

## Review

The procedure is reviewed before each field season. Changes are tested in a drill and then added to the next version. The current version took effect on 2024-10-25.
