# Winter Preparation Checklist

Category: Field operations
Date: 2026-10-01
Author: Caleb Finch
Source: Raptor Reserve internal record

Winter preparation starts on 15 October and must be finished by 15 November. The island gets freezing spray, short days, and occasional storm surges, so the checklist is strict.

The 12 tasks are: shut off and drain the visitor center water lines; switch the solar array to winter mode; service the generator; inspect the lighthouse roof; secure the boat shed; pull the docks at Seal Cove; store the trail signs; winterize the research station; check the radio repeater battery; stock the emergency cabin; move the camera traps to the lower forest; and review the storm plan with staff.

The maintenance team works with the head ranger and the veterinarian. The veterinarian checks the clinic's oxygen, drugs, and heating before the last ferry. The data manager confirms that the off-island backup is current.

The checklist is signed by the maintenance lead and the director. Any incomplete task needs a written exception and a date. The reserve keeps a copy in the maintenance shed and a copy in the board packet.

The first winter storm usually arrives in late November. The last ferry is scheduled for 20 November.

## Purpose and scope

Winter Preparation Checklist is the standard procedure for this part of the field program. It applies to staff, seasonal employees, and trained volunteers. It does not replace the safety plan (rr-005) or the emergency response plan (rr-006).

## Roles

The head ranger, Elin Ward, owns the procedure. The field lead assigns teams and checks the weather. The data manager, Samir Haddad, checks the records. The veterinarian, Dr. Priya Nair, is consulted for any wildlife handling.

## Procedure

1. Check the weather, the tide, and the radio.
2. Sign out at the research station and record the team.
3. Follow the route and the timing in the document.
4. Record the data in the field form before leaving the site.
5. Report any safety issue, injury, or unusual wildlife behavior to the head ranger.
6. Sign in at the end of the shift.

## Data and records

Records go into the field database within 24 hours. Each record needs date, time, observer, location, weather, and a confidence note. Photos are optional and should not include people without consent. The data manager checks the records before the weekly summary.

## Safety

Work in pairs. Carry a radio, a first aid kit, and water. Stay 150 m from the cliff edge at North Point. Do not fly a drone above 28 km/h wind or within 250 m of an active nest. Stop work in lightning, fog below 2 km visibility, or a red risk level.

## Why it matters

Consistent records are the only way to compare years. A missed field or a changed route can look like a population change. The reserve would rather lose a day of data than publish a number that cannot be defended.

## Review

The procedure is reviewed before each field season. Changes are tested in a drill and then added to the next version. The current version took effect on 2026-10-01.
