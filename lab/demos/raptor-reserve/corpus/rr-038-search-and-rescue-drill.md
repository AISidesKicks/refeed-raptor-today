# Search and Rescue Drill

Category: Field operations
Date: 2026-05-02
Author: Elin Ward
Source: Raptor Reserve internal record

The spring search and rescue drill took place on 2 May 2026. The scenario was a visitor who left the Granite Ridge trail at 11:00 and did not return to the visitor center by 15:00.

Fourteen staff took part. The duty officer set up the command post at the visitor center, assigned three search teams, and launched a drone. The teams covered the ridge trail, the north slope, and the service road. The drone flew a grid over the ridge and the upper forest.

The search found the "missing" visitor at 14:20, 600 m off trail, with a simulated ankle injury. The teams practiced packaging, carrying, and radio handoff. The whole drill took 3 hours and 10 minutes.

The after-action review found two problems: the trailhead sign still showed the winter hours, and the drone pilot had to wait 12 minutes for a battery. The sign was replaced the next day, and the drone kit now carries two spare batteries.

The next drill is the fall fire drill in October.

## Purpose and scope

Search and Rescue Drill is the standard procedure for this part of the field program. It applies to staff, seasonal employees, and trained volunteers. It does not replace the safety plan (rr-005) or the emergency response plan (rr-006).

## Roles

The head ranger, Elin Ward, owns the procedure. The field lead assigns teams and checks the weather. The data manager, Samir Haddad, checks the records. The veterinarian, Dr. Priya Nair, is consulted for any wildlife handling.

## Procedure

1. Check the weather, the tide, and the radio.
2. Sign out at the research station and record the team.
3. Follow the route and the timing in the document.
4. Record the data in the field form before leaving the site.
5. Report any safety issue, injury, or unusual wildlife behavior to the head ranger.
6. Sign in at the end of the shift.

## Data and records

Records go into the field database within 24 hours. Each record needs date, time, observer, location, weather, and a confidence note. Photos are optional and should not include people without consent. The data manager checks the records before the weekly summary.

## Safety

Work in pairs. Carry a radio, a first aid kit, and water. Stay 150 m from the cliff edge at North Point. Do not fly a drone above 28 km/h wind or within 250 m of an active nest. Stop work in lightning, fog below 2 km visibility, or a red risk level.

## Why it matters

Consistent records are the only way to compare years. A missed field or a changed route can look like a population change. The reserve would rather lose a day of data than publish a number that cannot be defended.

## Review

The procedure is reviewed before each field season. Changes are tested in a drill and then added to the next version. The current version took effect on 2026-05-02.
