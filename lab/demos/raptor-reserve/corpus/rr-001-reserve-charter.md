# Reserve Charter

Category: Governance
Date: 2024-01-15
Author: Board of Trustees
Source: Raptor Reserve internal record

Raptor Reserve is a nonprofit conservation area covering 4,820 hectares on Blacktail Island, about 18 km east of Sable Point, Maine. The reserve was founded in 1987 when the Harrow family transferred the island to the Raptor Reserve Trust. Its mission is to protect breeding and migratory raptors, maintain the island's coastal habitats, and support research and education that can be repeated by others.

The trust is governed by a nine-member board. The board appoints the director, approves the annual budget, and reviews the management plan every five years. Day-to-day operations are led by the director and the head ranger. The reserve has 9 full-time staff, 22 seasonal staff, and about 60 active volunteers.

The reserve does not allow hunting, trapping, off-road vehicles, or lead ammunition. Visitors may use the marked trails from 7:00 to 19:00 between May and October, and from 8:00 to 16:00 between November and April. Research permits are required for any work that disturbs vegetation, wildlife, or the island's archaeological sites.

The charter can be amended by a two-thirds vote of the board, after a public comment period of at least 30 days. The current charter was last amended on 15 January 2024.

## Scope

Reserve Charter is the top-level document for the reserve. It sets the mission, the board structure, and the amendment rule. The management plan (rr-002) and the specific policies sit below it. When two documents disagree, the charter wins.

## Roles and responsibilities

The board approves the document. The director, Dr. Mara Voss, owns implementation and reports exceptions to the board. The head ranger, Elin Ward, is the day-to-day contact for field staff. The data manager, Samir Haddad, keeps the signed copy in the document register and makes sure the shared drive has the current version.

## What staff should do

Read the document during onboarding. Ask the director or the head ranger if a situation is not covered. Record decisions that deviate from the document, including the reason and the date. Do not edit the shared copy. Send proposed changes to the data manager.

## Records and evidence

The reserve keeps the approved version, the board minutes, the public comment summary, and the annual review memo. Records are kept for 10 years. Personnel details are stored separately and are not part of the public corpus.

## Review and amendment

Review follows the schedule in the document. If the document does not set one, the default is every two years. Proposed changes go to the board packet 14 days before the meeting. A charter change needs a two-thirds vote; other documents need a simple majority.

## Common failure modes

The most common problems are outdated copies, unclear ownership, and field decisions that are never written down. The reserve fixes these by naming one owner, using a single document register, and reviewing the field log at the Monday safety meeting.
