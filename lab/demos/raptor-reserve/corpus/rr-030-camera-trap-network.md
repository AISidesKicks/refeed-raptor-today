# Camera Trap Network

Category: Field operations
Date: 2026-04-01
Author: Tomas Reed
Source: Raptor Reserve internal record

The reserve operates 20 camera traps on a roughly 2 km grid. The cameras are placed on trails, nest access routes, and known perch sites. They are not placed inside nest territories during the breeding season.

Each camera is deployed for 30 days. Staff check it every 7 days to replace batteries and download images. The camera records date, time, temperature, and moon phase. Images are tagged with species, number of individuals, behavior, and confidence.

In 2025 the network logged 4,100 trap nights and 1,240 raptor detections, or 0.30 detections per 100 trap nights. Red-tailed hawks were the most common detection, followed by barred owls and northern harriers. The network also recorded fox, deer, mink, and one otter.

Camera traps are useful for presence and behavior, not for abundance. The reserve combines them with the nest survey and the migration count to get a fuller picture.

Images of people are deleted unless they are part of a safety incident. The privacy policy in rr-098 applies.

## Purpose and scope

Camera Trap Network is the standard procedure for this part of the field program. It applies to staff, seasonal employees, and trained volunteers. It does not replace the safety plan (rr-005) or the emergency response plan (rr-006).

## Roles

The head ranger, Elin Ward, owns the procedure. The field lead assigns teams and checks the weather. The data manager, Samir Haddad, checks the records. The veterinarian, Dr. Priya Nair, is consulted for any wildlife handling.

## Procedure

1. Check the weather, the tide, and the radio.
2. Sign out at the research station and record the team.
3. Follow the route and the timing in the document.
4. Record the data in the field form before leaving the site.
5. Report any safety issue, injury, or unusual wildlife behavior to the head ranger.
6. Sign in at the end of the shift.

## Data and records

Records go into the field database within 24 hours. Each record needs date, time, observer, location, weather, and a confidence note. Photos are optional and should not include people without consent. The data manager checks the records before the weekly summary.

## Safety

Work in pairs. Carry a radio, a first aid kit, and water. Stay 150 m from the cliff edge at North Point. Do not fly a drone above 28 km/h wind or within 250 m of an active nest. Stop work in lightning, fog below 2 km visibility, or a red risk level.

## Why it matters

Consistent records are the only way to compare years. A missed field or a changed route can look like a population change. The reserve would rather lose a day of data than publish a number that cannot be defended.

## Review

The procedure is reviewed before each field season. Changes are tested in a drill and then added to the next version. The current version took effect on 2026-04-01.
