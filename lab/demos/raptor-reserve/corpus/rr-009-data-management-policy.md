# Data Management Policy

Category: Governance
Date: 2025-09-01
Author: Samir Haddad
Source: Raptor Reserve internal record

The reserve owns the data collected on the island. Staff and researchers get a license to use the data for the purpose described in their permit.

All datasets need a named owner, a description, a date range, a license, and a retention period. The default retention period is 10 years. Nest locations are stored at reduced precision in public datasets. Visitor names, phone numbers, and payment details are never stored in the research database.

Backups follow the 3-2-1 rule: three copies, two media, one off-island. The primary store is a PostgreSQL database at the research station. The secondary is a nightly encrypted backup to the mainland office. The third is a monthly cold copy in a fireproof safe.

Sensitive data has a two-year embargo. After two years, the owner must either publish a summary or ask the board for an extension. The board can grant one extension of up to two years.

The data dictionary in document rr-096 lists the fields used by the corpus and the retrieval system.

## Scope

Data Management Policy applies to the board, the director, full-time and seasonal staff, volunteers, researchers, and contractors working on Blacktail Island. It does not replace the charter (rr-001), the emergency response plan (rr-006), or the safety plan (rr-005). When two documents disagree, the charter wins, then the management plan, then the specific policy.

## Roles and responsibilities

The board approves the document. The director, Dr. Mara Voss, owns implementation and reports exceptions to the board. The head ranger, Elin Ward, is the day-to-day contact for field staff. The data manager, Samir Haddad, keeps the signed copy in the document register and makes sure the shared drive has the current version.

## What staff should do

Read the document during onboarding. Ask the director or the head ranger if a situation is not covered. Record decisions that deviate from the document, including the reason and the date. Do not edit the shared copy. Send proposed changes to the data manager.

## Records and evidence

The reserve keeps the approved version, the board minutes, the public comment summary, and the annual review memo. Records are kept for 10 years. Personnel details are stored separately and are not part of the public corpus.

## Review and amendment

Review follows the schedule in the document. If the document does not set one, the default is every two years. Proposed changes go to the board packet 14 days before the meeting. A charter change needs a two-thirds vote; other documents need a simple majority.

## Common failure modes

The most common problems are outdated copies, unclear ownership, and field decisions that are never written down. The reserve fixes these by naming one owner, using a single document register, and reviewing the field log at the Monday safety meeting.
