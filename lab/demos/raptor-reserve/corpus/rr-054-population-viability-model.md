# Population Viability Model

Category: Research and monitoring
Date: 2026-08-15
Author: Tomas Reed
Source: Raptor Reserve internal record

The reserve built a 100-year population viability model for the island's raptor community. The model uses current survival, fecundity, and carrying capacity. It projects a 2 percent annual growth rate and an 8 percent probability of dropping below 20 breeding pairs within 100 years.

The most sensitive parameters are adult survival, fledging success, and lead exposure. A 5 percent drop in adult survival has a larger effect than a 5 percent drop in fecundity. A 10 percent increase in lead exposure moves the extinction risk to 14 percent.

The model is simple. It does not include density dependence, disease, or a catastrophic storm. It treats the island as closed, which is not true for juveniles. The results are a planning tool, not a prediction.

The reserve will update the model every five years with new survival and occupancy estimates. The code and the parameter table are in the data release. The 2027 research plan includes a sensitivity analysis for wind energy and climate change.

## Background

Population Viability Model is part of the reserve's long-term monitoring program. The question is whether the island's raptor population is changing, and whether the change is driven by weather, prey, disease, or human disturbance. The reserve has collected comparable data since 2016.

## Methods

The analysis uses the standard field protocols. Counts use six fixed stations and a four-hour window. Nest checks use three visits and a 150 m observation distance. Occupancy models use the 2016-2025 records and account for detection probability. The data manager checks the records before analysis.

## Results

The headline numbers are in the document. The full tables are in the annual data release. Estimates are reported with 95 percent confidence intervals. A difference is treated as meaningful only when the intervals do not overlap and the field effort was similar.

## What changed since the last report

The previous report used the same routes and the same three-visit protocol, so the comparison is direct. The 2026 analysis adds the new South Beach boardwalk data and the two acoustic stations installed in 2025. The older records are flagged where the effort or the observer changed. A difference that comes from a new route is not treated as a population change.

## Discussion

The reserve reads the results against the management plan. A single year is not a trend. A change that appears in one species but not in its prey or habitat is treated with caution. The team also checks whether a new observer, a new route, or a new sensor could explain the pattern.
