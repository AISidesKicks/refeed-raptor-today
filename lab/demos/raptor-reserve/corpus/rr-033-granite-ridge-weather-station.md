# Granite Ridge Weather Station

Category: Field operations
Date: 2026-01-20
Author: Caleb Finch
Source: Raptor Reserve internal record

The Granite Ridge Weather Station sits at 112 m on Granite Ridge. It records temperature, relative humidity, barometric pressure, wind speed, wind direction, solar radiation, and rainfall every 10 minutes.

The station has two wind sensors, one at 3 m and one at 10 m. The 10 m sensor is the official one for drone and safety decisions. The 3 m sensor is a backup and a check on shelter effects.

Staff clean the rain gauge every month and replace the desiccant every three months. The anemometer bearings are replaced every two years. The data logger has a battery backup and a solar panel.

In 2025 the station had 99.2 percent uptime. The longest gap was 11 hours after a lightning strike in July. The missing data was filled from the backup sensor and flagged in the database.

The station is part of a regional weather network. Data is shared hourly with the Maine Coast Trust and is available in the reserve's open data portal. The wind limit for drone flights is based on the 10 m sensor.

## Purpose and scope

Granite Ridge Weather Station is the standard procedure for this part of the field program. It applies to staff, seasonal employees, and trained volunteers. It does not replace the safety plan (rr-005) or the emergency response plan (rr-006).

## Roles

The head ranger, Elin Ward, owns the procedure. The field lead assigns teams and checks the weather. The data manager, Samir Haddad, checks the records. The veterinarian, Dr. Priya Nair, is consulted for any wildlife handling.

## Procedure

1. Check the weather, the tide, and the radio.
2. Sign out at the research station and record the team.
3. Follow the route and the timing in the document.
4. Record the data in the field form before leaving the site.
5. Report any safety issue, injury, or unusual wildlife behavior to the head ranger.
6. Sign in at the end of the shift.

## Data and records

Records go into the field database within 24 hours. Each record needs date, time, observer, location, weather, and a confidence note. Photos are optional and should not include people without consent. The data manager checks the records before the weekly summary.

## Safety

Work in pairs. Carry a radio, a first aid kit, and water. Stay 150 m from the cliff edge at North Point. Do not fly a drone above 28 km/h wind or within 250 m of an active nest. Stop work in lightning, fog below 2 km visibility, or a red risk level.

## Why it matters

Consistent records are the only way to compare years. A missed field or a changed route can look like a population change. The reserve would rather lose a day of data than publish a number that cannot be defended.

## Review

The procedure is reviewed before each field season. Changes are tested in a drill and then added to the next version. The current version took effect on 2026-01-20.
