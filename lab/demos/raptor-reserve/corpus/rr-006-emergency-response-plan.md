# Emergency Response Plan

Category: Governance
Date: 2026-02-15
Author: Elin Ward
Source: Raptor Reserve internal record

The emergency response plan covers medical emergencies, fire, lost visitors, severe weather, oil spills, and disease outbreaks in wildlife.

For a medical emergency, call 911, then switch to radio channel 3 and notify the duty officer. The Coast Guard responds on channel 16. The assembly point is the Seal Cove Visitor Center. The goal is to move visitors to the assembly point within 30 minutes and to evacuate the island within 2 hours if the ferry is available.

The island has three sirens: one at the visitor center, one at the research station, and one at Seal Cove. A continuous siren means evacuate to the assembly point. A pulsing siren means shelter in place.

For a lost visitor, the duty officer assigns a search team of at least four people. The team checks the trail log, the camera traps, and the last known location. The coast guard is notified after 60 minutes if the visitor is not found.

For an oil spill, staff stop the source, place absorbent pads, and call the state spill line. For a wildlife disease outbreak, staff wear gloves, double-bag carcasses, and call the veterinarian.

## Scope

Emergency Response Plan applies to the board, the director, full-time and seasonal staff, volunteers, researchers, and contractors working on Blacktail Island. It does not replace the charter (rr-001), the emergency response plan (rr-006), or the safety plan (rr-005). When two documents disagree, the charter wins, then the management plan, then the specific policy.

## Roles and responsibilities

The board approves the document. The director, Dr. Mara Voss, owns implementation and reports exceptions to the board. The head ranger, Elin Ward, is the day-to-day contact for field staff. The data manager, Samir Haddad, keeps the signed copy in the document register and makes sure the shared drive has the current version.

## What staff should do

Read the document during onboarding. Ask the director or the head ranger if a situation is not covered. Record decisions that deviate from the document, including the reason and the date. Do not edit the shared copy. Send proposed changes to the data manager.

## Records and evidence

The reserve keeps the approved version, the board minutes, the public comment summary, and the annual review memo. Records are kept for 10 years. Personnel details are stored separately and are not part of the public corpus.

## Review and amendment

Review follows the schedule in the document. If the document does not set one, the default is every two years. Proposed changes go to the board packet 14 days before the meeting. A charter change needs a two-thirds vote; other documents need a simple majority.

## Common failure modes

The most common problems are outdated copies, unclear ownership, and field decisions that are never written down. The reserve fixes these by naming one owner, using a single document register, and reviewing the field log at the Monday safety meeting.
