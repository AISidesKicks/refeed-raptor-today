# Spring Migration Count Protocol

Category: Field operations
Date: 2026-03-01
Author: Elin Ward
Source: Raptor Reserve internal record

The spring migration count runs from 15 March to 31 May. Counts start 30 minutes after sunrise and last four hours. The reserve counts on three days each week, usually Tuesday, Thursday, and Saturday, plus one weekend day when volunteers are available.

There are six count stations: North Point, Granite Ridge, Heron Marsh, Seal Cove, South Beach, and the west edge of Blacktail Forest. Each station has two observers. One observer scans with binoculars while the other records. They switch every 30 minutes.

Counts are cancelled when wind is above 20 km/h, visibility is below 2 km, or rain is steady. Light drizzle is acceptable if the birds are moving. Observers record species, number, direction, height band, behavior, and weather every hour.

The data goes into the migration table the same day. The count coordinator checks for duplicate entries and obvious species errors before the weekly summary. Raptors that are too distant to identify are recorded as "unidentified raptor" and are not included in the species totals.

The protocol is the same each year. Changes need approval from the research committee.

## Purpose and scope

Spring Migration Count Protocol is the standard procedure for this part of the field program. It applies to staff, seasonal employees, and trained volunteers. It does not replace the safety plan (rr-005) or the emergency response plan (rr-006).

## Roles

The head ranger, Elin Ward, owns the procedure. The field lead assigns teams and checks the weather. The data manager, Samir Haddad, checks the records. The veterinarian, Dr. Priya Nair, is consulted for any wildlife handling.

## Procedure

1. Check the weather, the tide, and the radio.
2. Sign out at the research station and record the team.
3. Follow the route and the timing in the document.
4. Record the data in the field form before leaving the site.
5. Report any safety issue, injury, or unusual wildlife behavior to the head ranger.
6. Sign in at the end of the shift.

## Data and records

Records go into the field database within 24 hours. Each record needs date, time, observer, location, weather, and a confidence note. Photos are optional and should not include people without consent. The data manager checks the records before the weekly summary.

## Safety

Work in pairs. Carry a radio, a first aid kit, and water. Stay 150 m from the cliff edge at North Point. Do not fly a drone above 28 km/h wind or within 250 m of an active nest. Stop work in lightning, fog below 2 km visibility, or a red risk level.

## Why it matters

Consistent records are the only way to compare years. A missed field or a changed route can look like a population change. The reserve would rather lose a day of data than publish a number that cannot be defended.

## Review

The procedure is reviewed before each field season. Changes are tested in a drill and then added to the next version. The current version took effect on 2026-03-01.
