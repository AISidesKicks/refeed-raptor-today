# Management Plan 2026-2035

Category: Governance
Date: 2026-01-05
Author: Dr. Mara Voss
Source: Raptor Reserve internal record

The 2026-2035 management plan sets five priorities for Raptor Reserve.

1. Protect at least 90 percent of occupied raptor territories. The baseline is the 2025 breeding season, when 38 of 46 monitored territories were occupied.
2. Keep lead exposure below 10 percent of sampled raptors. The 2024 case involving the peregrine Juniper showed that the ban on lead ammunition is not enough on its own.
3. Maintain 9 km of trails and 4 km of access road without expanding the footprint.
4. Complete the North Point climate monitoring program and publish a 10-year report by 2030.
5. Grow the endowment to USD 6 million by 2035 so the reserve depends less on annual grants.

The plan uses a 10-year horizon but is reviewed every five years. The board will measure progress with the same indicators used in the annual research report: occupancy, fledging success, lead exposure, visitor numbers, and maintenance backlog.

The plan also commits the reserve to an open data policy. Every research dataset must have a named owner, a retention period, and a public summary. Sensitive nest locations are the only exception; they are stored at reduced precision.

## Scope

Management Plan 2026-2035 applies to the board, the director, full-time and seasonal staff, volunteers, researchers, and contractors working on Blacktail Island. It does not replace the charter (rr-001), the emergency response plan (rr-006), or the safety plan (rr-005). When two documents disagree, the charter wins, then the management plan, then the specific policy.

## Roles and responsibilities

The board approves the document. The director, Dr. Mara Voss, owns implementation and reports exceptions to the board. The head ranger, Elin Ward, is the day-to-day contact for field staff. The data manager, Samir Haddad, keeps the signed copy in the document register and makes sure the shared drive has the current version.

## What staff should do

Read the document during onboarding. Ask the director or the head ranger if a situation is not covered. Record decisions that deviate from the document, including the reason and the date. Do not edit the shared copy. Send proposed changes to the data manager.

## Records and evidence

The reserve keeps the approved version, the board minutes, the public comment summary, and the annual review memo. Records are kept for 10 years. Personnel details are stored separately and are not part of the public corpus.

## Review and amendment

Review follows the schedule in the document. If the document does not set one, the default is every two years. Proposed changes go to the board packet 14 days before the meeting. A charter change needs a two-thirds vote; other documents need a simple majority.

## Common failure modes

The most common problems are outdated copies, unclear ownership, and field decisions that are never written down. The reserve fixes these by naming one owner, using a single document register, and reviewing the field log at the Monday safety meeting.
