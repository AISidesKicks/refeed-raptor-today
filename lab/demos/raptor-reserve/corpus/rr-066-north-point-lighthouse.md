# North Point Lighthouse

Category: Infrastructure and logistics
Date: 2026-03-01
Author: Caleb Finch
Source: Raptor Reserve internal record

The North Point lighthouse was built in 1892 and decommissioned in 1968. The tower is 23 m tall and sits on a granite foundation 40 m above the sea. It is listed on the state historic register.

The reserve restored the tower in 2019 with a USD 48,000 grant from the state historic preservation office. The work replaced the roof, repaired the masonry, and rebuilt the lantern room windows. The light is not active; the Coast Guard uses a modern buoy at The Narrows.

The tower is open for guided tours from June to September. Tours are limited to eight people and require a waiver. The stairs are steep and the lantern room is not accessible. Visitors with a fear of heights should wait at the base.

The 2026 project replaces six window panes damaged by storm Oscar and repaints the exterior railing. The cost is USD 7,400. The work is scheduled for May, before the tour season.

The maintenance team inspects the tower after every storm above 80 km/h.

## Risks

The main risks are weather, salt corrosion, wildlife damage, and supply delays. The reserve keeps a storm kit, a spare parts bin, and a contingency line in the budget. Any outage that affects safety is reported to the director the same day.

## Winter
