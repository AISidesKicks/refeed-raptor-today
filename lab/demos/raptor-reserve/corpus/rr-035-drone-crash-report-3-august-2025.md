# Drone Crash Report, 3 August 2025

Category: Field operations
Date: 2025-08-05
Author: Elin Ward
Source: Raptor Reserve internal record

On 3 August 2025 at 10:42, a reserve drone crashed in Heron Marsh during a nest survey. The pilot was Caleb Finch. The drone was 180 m from the pilot at 42 m altitude when the battery voltage dropped from 48 percent to 21 percent in less than 20 seconds. The drone entered a forced landing and hit the marsh.

No birds were harmed. The nearest active nest was 310 m away. The drone was recovered after a two-hour search. The camera and the flight log survived. The replacement cost was USD 2,100.

The cause was battery failure. The battery had 94 charge cycles and had shown a small voltage sag on the previous flight. The maintenance log had not flagged it.

Corrective actions: retire drone batteries after 80 cycles, check voltage under load before every flight, and add a pre-flight battery health field to the drone book. The reserve also bought a battery analyzer and now keeps a spare battery at the marsh station.

The report was reviewed by the safety committee on 11 August 2025. The committee closed the case with all three actions complete.

## Purpose and scope

Drone Crash Report, 3 August 2025 is the standard procedure for this part of the field program. It applies to staff, seasonal employees, and trained volunteers. It does not replace the safety plan (rr-005) or the emergency response plan (rr-006).

## Roles

The head ranger, Elin Ward, owns the procedure. The field lead assigns teams and checks the weather. The data manager, Samir Haddad, checks the records. The veterinarian, Dr. Priya Nair, is consulted for any wildlife handling.

## Procedure

1. Check the weather, the tide, and the radio.
2. Sign out at the research station and record the team.
3. Follow the route and the timing in the document.
4. Record the data in the field form before leaving the site.
5. Report any safety issue, injury, or unusual wildlife behavior to the head ranger.
6. Sign in at the end of the shift.

## Data and records

Records go into the field database within 24 hours. Each record needs date, time, observer, location, weather, and a confidence note. Photos are optional and should not include people without consent. The data manager checks the records before the weekly summary.

## Safety

Work in pairs. Carry a radio, a first aid kit, and water. Stay 150 m from the cliff edge at North Point. Do not fly a drone above 28 km/h wind or within 250 m of an active nest. Stop work in lightning, fog below 2 km visibility, or a red risk level.

## Why it matters

Consistent records are the only way to compare years. A missed field or a changed route can look like a population change. The reserve would rather lose a day of data than publish a number that cannot be defended.

## Review

The procedure is reviewed before each field season. Changes are tested in a drill and then added to the next version. The current version took effect on 2025-08-05.
